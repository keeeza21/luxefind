Gift Card Promotions: The Fine Print Most Luxury Shoppers Miss

A gift card promotion — "spend $500, get a $100 gift card" — reads like free money, and in a lot of cases it genuinely is. But the structure of these promotions has a few consistent quirks that are worth understanding before you plan a purchase around one.

Thresholds are usually tiered, not flat

Most luxury retailers structure gift card events as a ladder: spend $150 and get $25, spend $500 and get $100, spend $1,500 and get $300, and so on. The percentage return generally improves the more you spend, which is intentional — it's designed to nudge a shopper who is $80 short of the next tier into adding one more item. If you're close to a threshold, it's worth checking whether one additional item actually gets you into better relative value before checking out.

The gift card almost always excludes what you'd expect

Fragrance, beauty, gift cards themselves, and a handful of "excluded designer" lines typically don't count toward the qualifying spend, even when the rest of the site is included. This is the single most common reason someone's cart total doesn't trigger the tier they expected — a $520 cart with $150 of excluded fragrance may only count as $370 toward the promotion.

Expiration windows are shorter than people assume

Issued gift cards commonly expire 30 to 60 days after issue, and the retailer typically will not extend that window. Because the card is often issued a few days after the original purchase (not instantly), it's easy to lose track of when the clock actually started. If a gift card promotion overlaps with an upcoming sale you're already planning to shop, that's the ideal way to use it before it expires.

Can you stack it with a percentage-off sale?

In most cases, yes — the gift card mechanic and a Friends & Family percentage discount are separate systems, and qualifying spend is usually calculated on the discounted total rather than blocking the stack entirely. That said, always confirm on the specific retailer's terms page for that event, since exclusions and stacking rules are set independently each season.
