How Friends & Family Sales Actually Work at Neiman Marcus, Saks, and Bergdorf Goodman

If you've worked in luxury retail for any length of time, you've had this conversation a dozen times: a customer asks "when's the next sale?" as if there's one universal answer. There isn't. Each department store runs Friends & Family on its own calendar, with its own exclusions, and the fine print is where most shoppers lose money without realizing it.

The discount is rarely sitewide

Friends & Family language like "up to 25% off" almost always comes with a category carve-out. Fine jewelry, beauty, and a rotating list of "excluded designers" (frequently Chanel, Hermès, and certain Gucci or Louis Vuitton lines) sit outside the promotion because those brands control their own pricing agreements with the retailer. Before you plan a purchase around a sale window, check the specific brand against that season's exclusion list — it's usually buried in the sale's terms page, not the marketing banner.

Why timing within the window matters

Inventory for the best sizes and colorways in handbags and ready-to-wear moves fastest in the first 48 hours of any Friends & Family event. Department stores stagger email invitations to loyalty tiers before the public sale date, which means by the time a sale is publicly announced, top-tier cardholders may have already had a few days' head start. If you're chasing a specific item rather than browsing generally, the safest play is to check the retailer's site the moment the public window opens rather than waiting a week.

Gift card promotions are a separate lever

Gift card events (spend $X, get a $Y gift card) are a different mechanic from the percentage-off sale and the two often overlap without being explicitly the same threshold. The gift card is typically issued electronically within a few days and can usually be combined with the percentage-off discount, effectively stacking savings — but the gift card almost always excludes the same categories the main sale excludes, and it has its own expiration window (commonly 30-60 days), so don't let it sit unused.

What this means practically

The most reliable way to actually capture these savings is to track live windows across stores rather than relying on any single retailer's newsletter, since the best relative deal on a given week shifts between Saks, Neiman Marcus, Bergdorf Goodman, and Bloomingdale's depending on where each is in its own promotional calendar.
